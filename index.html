01 / FEATURED RESEARCH

One research question, tested from three angles.

When an alignment intervention looks coherent internally, when can we trust it to behave consistently across languages and tasks?

Characterise the failure → explain where it comes from → test whether alignment remains reliable beyond English.

MSc Dissertation · 2026

Cross-Lingual Activation Steering in LLMs

Evaluation languages: English, Italian, Chinese, Korean and Kazakh

Tested whether English-derived Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) steering transfers across three model families, and whether cross-lingual representation geometry predicts behavioural change.

I ran the experiments on HPC, designed layer-wise evaluations, and used bootstrap confidence intervals plus Gaussian and permutation random-vector baselines to separate systematic effects from noise.

KEY FINDING Representation similarity ≠ behavioural reliability.

Qwen and Gemma showed robust cross-lingual sycophancy transfer, while OLMo3 exposed a clearer geometry–behaviour mismatch. Across models, cosine similarity was not a reliable predictor of effect magnitude.

Collaborative Research · Ongoing

Cross-Lingual & Cross-Task Generalisation of Alignment Interventions

This follow-up grows directly from the failure modes I identified in my dissertation. I am extending the question from cross-lingual transfer to cross-task generalisation and using those failures to define the next mechanistic tests.

CURRENT WORK Cross-task transfer experiments are underway; probing, subspace analysis and causal localisation are planned to distinguish metric limitation, subspace mismatch and downstream divergence.

My role: carrying the dissertation’s representation–behaviour gap into the next experimental questions and testing which explanation survives broader transfer settings.

Research Project · Ongoing

Multilingual Safety Across OLMo 3 Alignment Stages

I built a reproducible evaluation pipeline for SFT, DPO and RLVR checkpoints to test whether post-training safety gains remain consistent across languages rather than looking reliable only in English.

PILOT RESULT A 50-item parallel English–Korean pilot found a consistent harmful-request refusal gap between the two languages across all three post-training stages; larger-scale evaluation is ongoing.

What I built: typed experiment/result schemas, benchmark-native scoring, deterministic parallel-sample validation, GitHub Actions CI, and A40 evaluation runs.
