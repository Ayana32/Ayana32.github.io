MSc Dissertation · 2026

Cross-Lingual Activation Steering in LLMs

Evaluation languages: English, Italian, Chinese, Korean and Kazakh

Why: Activation steering can modify LLM behaviour without retraining, but it is unclear whether English-derived interventions generalise reliably across languages and tasks.

How: Evaluated Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) across three model families and five languages on HPC, using layer-wise interventions, held-out evaluations, bootstrap confidence intervals, and Gaussian and permutation random-vector baselines.

KEY FINDING Representation similarity ≠ behavioural reliability.

High representational similarity did not guarantee comparable behavioural effects. Qwen transferred sycophancy but not truthfulness, while OLMo showed the opposite pattern. This establishes that representational similarity alone is not sufficient; whether it is necessary remains unresolved.

Cross-Lingual & Cross-Task Generalisation of Alignment Interventions

Why: Similar steering representations do not consistently predict behavioural transfer, leaving open whether differences arise from steering-vector strength, direction, evaluation metrics, or target-language behaviour.

CURRENT WORK Follow-up analyses have examined norm matching, directional coherence, and alternative transfer metrics. The joint manuscript is in preparation (2027); further mechanistic explanations remain open research questions.

How / my role: Extended transfer evaluations and compared source-vector norms, directional coherence, and behavioural metrics to investigate possible explanations for uneven transfer.

Research Project · Ongoing

Multilingual Safety Across OLMo 3 Alignment Stages

Why: Safety improvements measured in English may not generalise to other languages, and automated safety judges can introduce language-dependent measurement errors.

KEY FINDINGS For matched English and Korean harmful requests, scorer-measured refusal rates were approximately 80% versus 10%, with a large gap persisting under human validation. Human annotation also revealed safety-judge errors in opposite directions across the two languages.

How: Built a reproducible framework covering OLMo 3 post-training checkpoints (SFT, DPO and RLVR), evaluated 200 matched prompts per language, and performed stratified human validation of 70 responses with bootstrap and Bayesian uncertainty analysis.

Applied AI Project · 2026

Sustainability Report Compliance Checker

  • Why: ESG compliance verification needs traceable evidence, but retrieval failures can undermine automated judgements.
  • Finding: Hybrid retrieval with reranking improved Hit@5 from 83.3% to 100% and MRR from 0.628 to 0.819 over BM25-only retrieval.
  • How: Built and audited a retrieval and verification pipeline over 11 ESG reports, using a manually annotated gold set and retrieval-error analysis; deployed with FastAPI, Streamlit and Docker.
